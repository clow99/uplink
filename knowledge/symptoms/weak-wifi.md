# Weak Wi-Fi Signal

You have internet service, but the Wi-Fi signal is too weak in parts of your home. Pages load slowly or not at all in certain rooms, and you may see only one or two signal bars on your device.

## Is it a Wi-Fi problem or a service problem?

Test your speed right next to the router. If speeds are good near the router but poor far away, the issue is Wi-Fi coverage, not your internet service.

## Common causes

### Distance and layout
- **Too far from the router**: Wi-Fi signal weakens with distance, especially through walls, floors, and ceilings.
- **Building materials**: concrete, brick, metal, and plaster walls absorb Wi-Fi significantly. Glass and wood are easier to penetrate.
- **Multi-story homes**: signal drops substantially between floors.

### Router issues
- **Poor placement**: router on the floor, in a closet, or in a corner of the house instead of a central elevated location.
- **Old router**: older routers have weaker radios and fewer antennas. Wi-Fi 4 (802.11n) routers have noticeably shorter range than Wi-Fi 5 or 6 routers.
- **Wrong band**: the 2.4 GHz band has much longer range than 5 GHz. If your device is on 5 GHz far from the router, try switching to 2.4 GHz.

### Interference
- **Other electronics**: microwaves, baby monitors, and cordless phones on the 2.4 GHz band cause interference.
- **Neighboring networks**: in apartments and dense neighborhoods, dozens of nearby Wi-Fi networks compete for the same channels.
- **Large metal objects**: refrigerators, filing cabinets, and mirrors reflect and block Wi-Fi signals.

## What to try

1. **Move the router**: place it in a central, elevated location — on a shelf or mounted on a wall, not on the floor or in a closet.
2. **Switch bands**: connect to 2.4 GHz for distant rooms (slower but longer range) or 5 GHz for nearby rooms (faster but shorter range).
3. **Change Wi-Fi channel**: use your router's admin page to select a less congested channel. Many routers have an auto-channel feature.
4. **Update firmware**: router updates often include Wi-Fi performance improvements.
5. **Add coverage**: for large homes, consider a mesh Wi-Fi system or a wired access point in the weak area.
6. **Remove interference**: keep the router away from microwaves and other 2.4 GHz devices.
