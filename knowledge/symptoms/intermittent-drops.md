# Intermittent Internet Drops

Your internet keeps cutting out and coming back. This is one of the most frustrating issues because it can be hard to pin down.

## Is it your Wi-Fi or your internet service?

The most important question: does the disconnection happen on Wi-Fi only, or does a wired (Ethernet) connection also drop?

- **Wi-Fi drops but Ethernet stays connected**: the issue is your router or Wi-Fi environment, not your ISP service.
- **Both Wi-Fi and Ethernet drop**: the issue is likely your modem, cabling, or ISP service.

## Common causes

### Equipment-related
- **Overheating modem or router**: enclosed spaces, stacked devices, or dust buildup cause overheating and automatic restarts. Feel the device — if it's hot to the touch, improve ventilation.
- **Loose cables**: coax, Ethernet, or power cables that aren't fully seated can cause intermittent signal loss. Push each cable firmly and check for damage.
- **Outdated firmware**: router or modem software bugs can cause periodic disconnections. Check for updates in the device admin panel.

### Wi-Fi-related
- **Interference**: microwaves, baby monitors, cordless phones, and neighbor Wi-Fi networks on the same channel can disrupt your signal.
- **Channel congestion**: too many networks on the same Wi-Fi channel. Try switching to a less crowded channel or use the 5 GHz band.
- **DFS channel radar events**: if your router uses DFS channels (5 GHz), it must vacate the channel when it detects radar, causing brief drops.

### Service-related
- **Peak-hour congestion**: cable internet can slow down or become unstable during 6-10 PM when many people in your area are online.
- **ISP line issues**: degraded signal from the street to your home, loose connections at the junction box, or damaged wiring.
- **Partial outage**: intermittent service disruption in your area that hasn't been fully detected yet.

## What to try

1. **Check cables**: push all cables firmly into their ports. Replace any that look damaged or bent.
2. **Ventilate equipment**: move modem and router to an open area. Don't stack them on top of each other.
3. **Restart equipment**: unplug modem for 30 seconds, plug back in, wait 5 minutes, then restart router.
4. **Test with Ethernet**: connect directly to the router to determine if the drops are Wi-Fi-specific.
5. **Check modem logs**: many modems keep event logs accessible at 192.168.100.1 that show connection drops and errors.
6. **Monitor the pattern**: note the time and duration of each drop. If it's always at the same time, it may be congestion or a scheduled process.
