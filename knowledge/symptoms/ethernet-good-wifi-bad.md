# Ethernet Works but Wi-Fi Doesn't

Your internet works fine when you plug in an Ethernet cable, but Wi-Fi is slow, drops out, or doesn't work at all. This confirms your ISP service and modem are working — the problem is with the wireless side of your network.

## Common causes

### Router Wi-Fi issues
- **Wi-Fi radio disabled**: the Wi-Fi may have been accidentally turned off via a physical button on the router, an admin setting, or a firmware update.
- **Wi-Fi channel congestion**: too many nearby networks on the same channel cause interference and slow speeds.
- **Router hardware failing**: the Wi-Fi radio in the router may be degrading while the wired ports still function normally.

### Configuration issues
- **Wrong Wi-Fi band**: you may be connected to the 2.4 GHz band (slower, longer range) instead of 5 GHz (faster, shorter range), or vice versa.
- **Bandwidth limits set**: some routers have per-device or per-band bandwidth limits configured in QoS or parental controls.
- **Too many connected devices**: the router may be overwhelmed by the number of Wi-Fi clients.

### Environment issues
- **Distance**: you're testing from too far away. Wi-Fi speed drops significantly with distance.
- **Interference**: microwave ovens, baby monitors, and other 2.4 GHz devices disrupt the signal.
- **Physical obstacles**: thick walls, metal surfaces, and water (like aquariums) block Wi-Fi.

## What to try

1. **Restart the router**: unplug for 30 seconds, plug back in. This clears the Wi-Fi radio state and memory.
2. **Check Wi-Fi is enabled**: look for a Wi-Fi button on the router or log into the admin panel to verify Wi-Fi is on.
3. **Test near the router**: stand within 10 feet of the router and test speed. If it's fast up close but slow far away, it's a range/coverage issue.
4. **Switch bands**: try connecting to the other band (2.4 GHz or 5 GHz) to see if one performs better.
5. **Change Wi-Fi channel**: in the router admin, try a different channel. For 2.4 GHz, channels 1, 6, and 11 are best. For 5 GHz, try channels with DFS support for less congestion.
6. **Update router firmware**: check the manufacturer's site or the admin panel for firmware updates.
7. **Factory reset**: if other steps fail, a factory reset of the router can resolve configuration issues. You'll need to set up your Wi-Fi name and password again.
8. **Consider replacement**: if the router is several years old and Wi-Fi keeps degrading, the wireless radio may be failing. Upgrading to a Wi-Fi 6 or mesh system can resolve the issue.
