# Fiber vs DSL vs Cable Internet

The type of internet connection you have affects your speed, reliability, and what equipment you need.

## Fiber Optic

Fiber uses thin glass strands to transmit data as light. It is the fastest and most reliable connection type available.

- **Download speed**: 100 Mbps to 10 Gbps
- **Upload speed**: typically symmetrical (same as download)
- **Latency**: very low (1-5 ms)
- **Reliability**: excellent — not affected by electrical interference or weather
- **Equipment**: ONT (Optical Network Terminal) where the fiber line enters your home, connected to a router
- **Limitation**: not available in all areas; installation may require running new cable to your home

## Cable Internet

Cable uses the same coaxial wiring as cable TV to deliver internet service.

- **Download speed**: 100 Mbps to 1.2 Gbps
- **Upload speed**: typically much lower than download (5-50 Mbps)
- **Latency**: low to moderate (10-30 ms)
- **Reliability**: good, but speeds can slow during peak hours because bandwidth is shared with nearby subscribers
- **Equipment**: cable modem connected to a coaxial wall outlet, then a router
- **Limitation**: upload speeds are significantly slower than download; peak-hour congestion can be noticeable

## DSL (Digital Subscriber Line)

DSL delivers internet over existing copper telephone lines.

- **Download speed**: 5-100 Mbps (varies greatly by distance from the provider's equipment)
- **Upload speed**: 1-10 Mbps
- **Latency**: moderate (20-50 ms)
- **Reliability**: fair — dedicated line (not shared), but performance degrades with distance
- **Equipment**: DSL modem connected to a phone jack, then a router
- **Limitation**: speed is heavily dependent on how far you are from the nearest DSLAM (provider equipment). Long distances mean slower speeds.

## Quick comparison

| Feature | Fiber | Cable | DSL |
|---------|-------|-------|-----|
| Max download | 10 Gbps | 1.2 Gbps | 100 Mbps |
| Upload speed | Symmetrical | Much lower | Much lower |
| Latency | Very low | Low-moderate | Moderate |
| Shared bandwidth | No | Yes | No |
| Availability | Limited | Wide | Wide |
| Best for | Everything | Streaming, general use | Light use, rural areas |

## How to tell what you have

- **Fiber**: look for a small box (ONT) where the cable enters your home. The cable is very thin and should not be bent sharply.
- **Cable**: you'll have a coaxial cable (round with a center pin) going to your modem.
- **DSL**: a phone-style cable (smaller than Ethernet) connects your modem to a phone jack.

If you're unsure, check your ISP account page or the label on your modem.
