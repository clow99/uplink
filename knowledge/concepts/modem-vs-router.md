# Modem vs Router

## Modem

A modem connects your home to your internet service provider (ISP). It translates the signal from the ISP's network (cable, fiber, or DSL) into a form your home network can use.

- Connects to the outside line (coax cable, fiber optic, or phone line).
- Usually has one Ethernet port to connect to a router.
- Has indicator lights showing power, connection status, and activity.
- If your modem is offline, you have no internet — even if your Wi-Fi is working.

## Router

A router creates your home network. It connects to the modem and shares that single internet connection with all your devices over Wi-Fi and Ethernet.

- Creates your Wi-Fi network (the network name and password you connect to).
- Assigns local IP addresses to each device.
- Can have multiple Ethernet ports for wired connections.
- If your router is off or broken, your Wi-Fi stops working, but the modem may still be online.

## Gateway (combo device)

Many ISPs provide a single device that is both a modem and a router. This is called a gateway or combo unit. It does both jobs in one box.

## Why this matters for troubleshooting

- If all devices (wired and wireless) have no internet, the modem or ISP service is usually the issue.
- If wired devices work but Wi-Fi does not, the router or Wi-Fi settings are the issue.
- If only one device has problems, the device itself is likely the issue.
