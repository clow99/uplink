# Wi-Fi Bands

Modern routers broadcast on multiple frequency bands. Understanding the differences helps you get the best performance.

## 2.4 GHz

- **Range**: longest — can cover a large home and pass through walls better.
- **Speed**: slower — typical real-world speeds of 50-100 Mbps.
- **Congestion**: most crowded — shared with microwaves, baby monitors, Bluetooth, and many neighbor networks.
- **Best for**: devices far from the router, smart home devices, older devices that only support 2.4 GHz.

## 5 GHz

- **Range**: medium — shorter than 2.4 GHz, more affected by walls.
- **Speed**: faster — real-world speeds of 200-800+ Mbps depending on the router and device.
- **Congestion**: less crowded than 2.4 GHz, especially on DFS channels.
- **Best for**: streaming, video calls, gaming, and any use near the router.

## 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7)

- **Range**: shortest — very sensitive to walls and distance.
- **Speed**: fastest — can exceed 1 Gbps in ideal conditions.
- **Congestion**: almost no congestion — this band is new and has few competing devices.
- **Best for**: high-bandwidth tasks very close to the router. Requires Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 capable devices.

## Which band should I use?

- **Close to the router**: use 5 GHz (or 6 GHz if available).
- **Far from the router**: use 2.4 GHz for reliability, or add a mesh node for 5 GHz coverage.
- **Smart home devices**: many (thermostats, bulbs, cameras) only support 2.4 GHz.
- **Gaming / streaming**: prefer 5 GHz or wired Ethernet for the lowest latency.

## How to tell which band you are on

If your router shows separate network names like "MyWiFi" and "MyWiFi-5G", you can choose directly. If it uses a single name with band steering, your device and router negotiate automatically. You can check your device's Wi-Fi connection details to see the current frequency.
